================================================================================
KARGO HIRING RUBRIC  -  PRODUCT MANAGER & SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER
Version 1.0  |  28 Sept 2026  |  Built from 8 past-hire CVs + 2 JDs
================================================================================

PURPOSE
Rank applicants by how closely they resemble Kargo's Exceeds-rated hires, not
by how many JD keywords they contain. JD matching has run for 11 weeks and
produced zero offers. The JD describes the role. It does not predict who will
succeed in it.

THE ONE RULE
The rubric scores and recommends. Arjun decides. No candidate is rejected by
the rubric alone. Low scorers go into a decline queue that Arjun approves in
a single batch, and every applicant hears back.

SCORING ORDER
Gates -> 5 scored criteria -> Hidden-value notes -> Probes -> Band

LIMITS OF THIS ANALYSIS  (read before trusting any score)
  [Certain] 8 hires, only 2 of them PMs. Patterns are drawn across functions.
  [Certain] Built from CVs only. The hire profiles with interview notes and
            outcome notes described in the brief were not provided.
  [Certain] Calibration in Section 10 is in-sample: the patterns were drawn
            from these 8 people, so of course they fit. It shows consistency,
            not predictive power.
  [Likely]  Lavanya (Apr 2025) and Rahul (Jun 2025) were rated on short
            tenure. Their ratings are less stable than the others'.
  [Certain] No past hire was at SPM level. The SPM version of Criterion 4 is
            JD-derived and unvalidated.

SECTION 2  -  GATES  (pass / flag / fail. Not scored.)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

G1  EXPERIENCE FLOOR  (per role, not a flat 3-year rule)
  PM:   >= 2 years in a role where they owned product outcomes
        (PM, sole product owner, or founding-team product work).
        Near-miss FLAG (not fail): 1-2 yrs PM + >= 2 yrs hands-on ops (C1 >= 3).
  SPM:  >= 5 years PM, with at least one area owned without a senior PM
        above them making the calls.
        Near-miss FLAG: 3-5 yrs PM + C1 >= 3 + C2 >= 3 -> route to PM rubric.
  Upper end: do not auto-fail over-experienced candidates. Flag "possibly
  over-levelled" and let Arjun decide.
  Why not 3 years flat: the PM JD itself says 2-4 years, and a 3-year PM
  floor would have rejected Lavanya.

G2  LOCATION  (in-office Mumbai, relocation OK per JD)
  Never infer from the CV. A Bengaluru address is not a "no." Mark
  "CONFIRM IN FIRST REPLY" and move on. Does not affect score.

G3  ROLE ROUTING
  SPM applicants who fail G1 but score high on C1-C3 -> rescore on PM rubric.
  PM applicants with 5+ yrs PM and C4-SPM evidence -> also score on SPM.
  Report both scores; Arjun picks.

FORMULA:  Total = sum over criteria of (score / 4) x weight

C1  LIVED THE CUSTOMER'S JOB  (25)
...............................................................................
Question: Has this person done the work Kargo's users do, with their own hands?

  0  No exposure to operations-heavy work.
  1  Adjacent from a desk: built, sold or integrated software for logistics
     or ops-heavy industries without doing the operational work.
  2  Hands-on ops in an adjacent industry (manufacturing, e-com fulfilment,
     field ops, warehousing) OR < 1 yr hands-on freight/logistics ops.
  3  1-3 yrs hands-on in freight forwarding, 3PL, port, customs or
     documentation; or deep commercial work embedded with ops teams.
  4  2+ yrs doing the forwarder/3PL job itself (documentation, carrier
     coordination, customs, exceptions) AND then carried it into a builder or
     functional role.

  Look for: B/L, SI, CoO, shipping bills, CHA, customs holds, DGFT, ICEGATE,
  CargoWise, carrier allocation, NDR, detention, berth windows, DO release,
  exception triage, shipments-per-month volumes.
  Does NOT count: "logistics" in an employer's name; tool names in a skills
  list with no role behind them; "domain knowledge" claims without a job.

  Probe: "Walk me through a bad morning in that job. What broke first, and
  what did you do before 10am?"

C2  LIVE-FIRE OWNERSHIP  (20)
...............................................................................
Question: When something broke in real time, did they personally fix it?

  0  No incident described.
  1  Mentions on-call, escalations or "fast-paced environment," no instance.
  2  A specific incident; they were part of the team response.
  3  A specific incident they personally drove to resolution under time
     pressure, with a stated outcome.
  4  Multiple such incidents, OR one where they also changed the system so it
     did not recur (post-mortem owned, process redesigned, monitoring added).

  Probe: "Tell me about the worst thing that broke on your watch. What did you
  do in the first hour? What did you change afterwards?"

C3  UNPROMPTED BUILD, ADOPTED BY OTHERS  (15)
...............................................................................
Question: Did they build something nobody asked for, that others chose to use?

  0  None.
  1  Improved something inside their assigned scope.
  2  Built a tool/process beyond scope; adoption only by own team or unstated.
  3  Built unprompted; adopted by people who didn't have to (peers, other
     teams, clients), with evidence ("became standard," numbers, timeline).
  4  Multiple instances, or one that became company- or client-wide standard.
     Bonus signal: started scrappy (Excel, weekend prototype) and upgraded as
     usage grew.

  Probe: "Who asked you to build that? How did the second team end up

C4  ROLE CAPABILITY  (25)  -  score the variant for the role being assessed
...............................................................................

C4-PM  (from PM JD: first PM, ship + kill in short cycles, build the rhythms)
  0  No product ownership evidence.
  1  Product-adjacent: APM or analyst who wrote specs for someone else's
     decisions.
  2  Owned features end-to-end in a structured org; shipped, but no evidence
     of killing or redirecting work, and no process built from scratch.
  3  Owned a product area; shipped multiple features with usage evidence;
     at least one kill/pivot based on data OR built a PM rhythm from nothing.
  4  Sole or first PM on an area at early stage; shipped AND killed in short
     cycles on usage data; discovery done with operators, ideally on-site;
     built the rhythms (prioritisation, templates, post-mortems).
  Key test: did users adopt features unprompted? That is Kargo's 6-month
  success metric. Mandated rollouts and "adoption %" with no baseline are weak.

  Probe: "What's something you shipped that users found without being told?
  What's something you killed, and what data made you kill it?"

C4-SPM  (from SPM JD: integration/data layer, build vs configure vs avoid)
  0  No PM ownership, or only UI-level features with no integration/data work.
  1  PM experience, always under a senior PM layer; no platform scope.
  2  Owned an area with some integration/API exposure (partner integrations,
     API docs) but no architectural calls.
  3  Owned an integration, platform or data area; clear build/configure/avoid
     decisions; worked with sales on integration-driven deals.
  4  All of 3, at an early stage with no committee above them; decisions with
     multi-year consequences they lived with; an integration that opened a
     segment or unblocked a deal; shaped how other PMs work.
  NOTE: unvalidated against past hires. Weight the probe answers heavily.

  Probe: "Name an integration you decided NOT to build. What did you do
  instead, and were you right 18 months later?"

...............................................................................
C5  LOW-STRUCTURE OPERATING RECORD  (15)  -  the "company fit" criterion
...............................................................................
Fit here means one thing only: evidence of doing good work where no structure
existed. It does NOT mean personality, background, or "would I get a beer
with them." Score behaviour, not employer size.

  0  All achievements inside layered orgs, framed as "part of" a team.
  1  Owned discrete pieces inside an established process.
  2  Growth-stage or small team; some ownership, but operating within
     processes others built.
  3  Sole owner of an area; reported to a founder/CEO or had no layer above;
     or ran independently (consulting, own book of business).
  4  Built something from zero with no template (first in role, created the
     function or process others now use) and says so plainly.

  Probe: "What did you have to build on day one because nothing existed?
  Who did you go to when you were stuck, and what if there was no one?"

SECTION 4  -  HIDDEN VALUE  (required field, not scored)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Things the JDs don't ask for but that would help Kargo. The scorer must list
any that appear, with the CV line that shows it. Arjun uses these to break
ties and to decide what to probe.

  - Regulatory/compliance fluency: DGFT, ICEGATE, customs EDI, RoDTEP, IATA DGR,
    CDSCO, WHO-GMP. Reduces documentation-workflow design risk.
  - Existing relationships: CHAs, shipping lines, terminal operators, carriers,
    forwarder ops teams. Shortens discovery and on-site access.
  - Led a customer off paper, Excel or WhatsApp onto software. This is
    literally Kargo's product motion.
  - Wrote customer-facing incident comms or post-mortems.
  - Wrote API or integration documentation (especially relevant for SPM).
  - Ran a "loss post-mortem" or killed their own work, documented why.
  - Worked on both sides of the table (ops user AND software builder/seller).
  - Onboarding or implementation experience at freight/3PL clients.
  - Mentored someone who was later promoted (with timeline).
  - Carrier/vendor performance scorecards or rate renegotiation from data.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5  -  WHAT NOT TO REWARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Neutral (neither add nor subtract):
  - Certifications and courses (Product School, Reforge, etc.)
  - Conference talks, communities, awards without a stated basis
  - School, MBA or employer brand
  - Tool lists (JIRA, Figma, Mixpanel) with no work attached

Caution flags (don't penalise, but mark for probe):
  - Every achievement is team-level ("part of," "contributed to") with no
    personal verb. Some people write modestly; probe before concluding.
  - Metrics with no baseline, time window, or named user problem.
  - "Managed stakeholders" with no decision they made.
  - Only large-company tenure AND no instance of acting outside process.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6  -  FAIRNESS RULES  (non-negotiable)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Never score, mention, or let influence a score:
  - Name, gender, age, date of birth, photo, marital status, religion, caste
  - College or school name (only degree field, if relevant)
  - Current city (relocation is confirmed by asking, not inferred)
  - Employment gaps (ask, don't assume)
  - Family business connections (score the WORK done there, not the family)
Risk to watch: Pattern 1 could narrow the pool to people from Mumbai port
networks. C1 score 2 exists so that strong operators from other industries
are not zeroed out. Review the gender and background mix of each shortlist
before Arjun sees it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7  -  EVIDENCE RULES FOR THE SCORER (AI or human)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Every score must quote the CV line it rests on. No quote -> score 0 and
     write "no evidence in CV." Never infer experience that isn't written.
  2. Tag each score's confidence: H (explicit, specific, quantified),
     M (explicit but vague), L (implied).
  3. "I built / owned / led" outranks "part of / supported / contributed."
  4. Specific > general. "Resolved a customs hold overnight" beats
     "strong problem-solver."
  5. Do not reward keyword density. Five mentions of "freight" is one fact.
  6. Score each criterion independently. Don't let a high C1 inflate C3.
  7. When unsure between two scores, pick the lower and add a probe.

SECTION 11  -  HOW TO VALIDATE AND WHEN TO CHANGE THIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before first use:
  1. Blind-score the 19 already-opened applications. Have Arjun rank the same
     19 on instinct without seeing scores. Review every case where the two
     disagree by more than one band. Each disagreement either finds a rubric
     gap or an instinct bias; both are worth knowing.
  2. Add the interview and outcome notes from the hires/ folder and
     re-derive the patterns. Specifically look for what Preetham's notes
     reveal.

Change the rubric if:
  - A shortlisted candidate interviews badly for a reason the rubric should
    have caught -> add or re-anchor a criterion.
  - Shortlists come back homogeneous (same city, same background) -> check
    C1 and the fairness review.
  - After 6 months, compare new hires' ratings to their rubric scores. That
    is the first real test of predictive power.

Do NOT change the weights because a single candidate "feels" wrong. That is
the gut-feel process this replaces.
